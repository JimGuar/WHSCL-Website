About WHSCL

Built for climbing.
Built for students.

The Wisconsin High School Climbing League gives high school students an alternative to conventional athletics: a sport where you compete against the wall and your own last attempt, alongside a team that gets you there.

Mission

Fun, active, social, climbing-centered.

The WHSCL provides a fun and welcoming environment, encourages an active lifestyle, builds social bonds, and promotes a love for the sport of rock climbing to high school students across the state of Wisconsin through competitions and weekly practices.

Vision

Empower students to climb.

To empower high school students to climb and to legitimize rock climbing as a competitive high school sport.

Core values

What the league protects.

Fun & welcoming

The WHSCL has been and always will be a welcoming environment for all Wisconsin high school students, regardless of race, religion, political belief, social status, gender, sexuality, or ability. That environment is built by the collective of coaches and by every student.

Active lifestyle

Physical fitness is central to climbing and demands real dedication. Because every student climbs at a different difficulty, each is encouraged to find an individual plan that matches their goals — ideally one that outlasts high school.

Community

A welcoming team leads to social bonds. The goal is not only teammates but friends. Climbing looks like an individual sport, but students rely on each other on and off the wall.

Love for rock climbing

Climbing is a lifelong activity, not just a sport. Some students gravitate to top rope, others to bouldering. Whichever discipline appeals, the aim is enjoyment of the sport as a whole.

Guiding principles

The Code of Conduct in five words.

Safety

Climbing carries real risk. The league prioritizes education, supervision, and safety protocols at all times.

Respect

We treat ourselves and others, our spaces, and our rules with dignity and care.

Sportsmanship

We compete with integrity, celebrate effort, support others, and show grace in victory and defeat.

Inclusivity

We welcome climbers of all backgrounds, abilities, and identities.

Responsibility

We hold ourselves accountable for our actions on and off the wall.

History

Three schools to a statewide league.

The league began as a marketing internship project and grew into a statewide organization spanning four climbing companies, two school walls, and three regions.

1998

Adventure Rock Brookfield opens

The Brookfield gym opens on February 16th.

2009

The league starts

Andy Miers founds the WHSCL through a UW–Milwaukee marketing internship with Adventure Rock. Three teams, about 30 students.

2016

Milwaukee opens

Adventure Rock’s second location opens June 18th, putting climbing within reach of Milwaukee-area schools.

2017

First State Finals

The inaugural State Finals closes the 2016–2017 season on May 12th with 95 students from 13 teams.

2020

Walker’s Point opens

Adventure Rock Walker’s Point, a dedicated bouldering gym, opens August 25th.

2023

Beyond Adventure Rock

Odyssey Climbing + Fitness Green Bay opens February 6th — the first WHSCL facility outside Adventure Rock — adding Northeast Wisconsin as a region. An official Handbook and Rulebook arrive that fall, and High School Hangouts begin.

2024

Scoring, Burlington, and Madison

Odyssey Green Bay hosts its first competition in March. A web-based scoring app launches for the 2024–2025 season. Climb @ the Loop joins from Burlington, and Greater Heights opens in Madison on December 2nd.

2025

Appleton and the Code of Conduct

Odyssey Climbing + Fitness opens a second location in Appleton on September 13th, and the league introduces its Code of Conduct.

2026

Two conferences

Odyssey Appleton hosts its first competition in January. Growth pushes the league to split into Conference A and Conference B so more students can compete.

2027

Madison hosts

Greater Heights hosts its first WHSCL competition on February 6th.
